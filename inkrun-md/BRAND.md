# Inkrun — Brand

Voice, copy rules, and product context. Load this before writing anything a reader will see.

---

## Product

Inkrun is a flat-rate digital publishing utility for small-town independent newspaper publishers in Canada. $35–$40 a month. No percentage cuts, no surprise fees. A publisher uploads the same print-ready PDF she sends to her printer and gets a clean web edition automatically. She owns the subscriber list, the payment data, and the reader relationships.

Domains: **inkrun.ca** (site) and **inkrun.pub**.

## Audience

The independent community news publisher. Tight margins, time-poor, technically hesitant but sharp. Her readers skew senior, and she knows exactly what they need because she has been serving them for years. Default persona: she/her.

She is being squeezed from two sides (see the villain narrative) and she is tired of being treated as someone else's inventory.

## Positioning

**We are a utility, never a landlord.**

That line carries the whole strategy. A utility charges a flat, boring price and stays out of your way. A landlord takes a cut of your business, raises the rent, and owns the ground under you. Every piece of copy should make the reader feel the difference. Three commitments back it up:

1. Flat rent — $35–$40 a month, the whole price.
2. You own the list — subscribers, payment data, relationships. Export any time.
3. You can leave any time — with everything.

## The villain narrative (in full)

Never name a company. Name the **behavior**, specifically, with figures. There are two villains:

**1. The legacy flipbook platform.**
Charges $2,000 or more a year for software that animates a PDF as a page-flip. The moment of no return is **opening the annual renewal invoice** — the same bill, bigger, for the same little. This is the story's opening scene.

**2. The grant-funded national "newsstand collective."**
Presents itself as free and noble. In practice: charges entry fees up front, skims an **"upkeep fee" out of the reader's pocket at checkout** (and the reader assumes the publisher set it), **blocks local ad sales** (the hardware store on Main Street is not allowed), and offers publishers **zero equity** in the thing their readers are paying for.

The three behaviors to name, always concretely: **the renewal bill, the fee skimmed at reader checkout, the blocked local advertiser.**

The emotional truth underneath: publishers have been paying these bills for years and feeling stupid about it. We say the quiet part out loud, calmly: *"We have paid those bills. We are done paying them."*

## Voice

A fellow publisher who has been burned. First person plural — *we, us, our* — because we are in the same boat, not selling from the dock. Quiet. Plain. The tone is **curiosity and respect**: we want to know who is out there; we do not push, qualify, or pressure.

## Copy rules

- Short declarative sentences. Active voice. Aim under 15 words, but natural rhythm beats rigid counting.
- **No exclamation points. Ever.** Calm is the point.
- No jargon. If a word belongs in a vendor pitch deck, it does not belong here.
- Name the villain's behavior with concrete figures (`$2,000 or more`, the upkeep fee, entry fees). Abstraction is how the villains get away with it.
- The moment of no return is the renewal invoice. Anchor problem copy there.
- Address the unspoken fear — leaving feels risky — in one short block, three sentences: migration is a PDF upload, not a project. Never more than one block; reassurance repeated too often sounds like doubt.
- No qualifying questions, no sales pressure, no countdown timers, no "limited spots."
- CASL-conscious: we only email about Inkrun, consent is explicit, unsubscribing is easy. The privacy line is fixed copy: **"We email you about Inkrun. Nothing else. Unsubscribe anytime."**
- Canadian spelling. The readers are Canadian; so is the product.

## Approved words and phrases

- flat rate · flat · the whole price
- utility (we are one) · never a landlord
- web edition · print-ready PDF
- readers · subscribers · your paper · community paper
- own · ownership · yours · export any time
- subscriber list · payment data · reader relationships
- press · ink · print shop · broadsheet · newsroom

## Banned words

- **leverage · solutions · streamline** (hard bans, SaaS pitch-deck)
- seamless · empower · ecosystem · synergy · utilize · disruptive · cutting-edge · best-in-class · all-in-one · one-stop · monetize
- **platform** — for ourselves. That is what the villains are. We are a utility. (The word may appear when describing *them*.)
- **content** — when we mean the paper. Say "the paper," "the edition," "the issue."
- Exclamation points count as banned words.
